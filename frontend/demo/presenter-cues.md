# Presenter cues — complete proposed 180-second judge story

**Status: every product beat below is implemented and browser-tested. The 180-second allocation is still a proposed spoken script until a human presents it aloud against a clock.** All data is fictional and labelled **Synthetic demo**. No payment, cancellation, application, message or provider call is performed.

## The headline

> Most AI finance demos generate a recommendation and ask you to trust it. ClauseGraph makes every recommendation carry its own evidence chain and bounded proof: exact source clause, human-reviewed permission, dependency, deterministic ledger effect, and either a verified-safe result or a concrete counterexample. The AI reads; deterministic code does the money; the verifier tries to break the plan before the user does.

Source baseline: merged consequence walkthrough `1251201`, plus the final C15 demo regression. The application—not these notes—is the source of every amount, date, proof status and evidence link.

## Six beats / 180 seconds

Budgets total **180 seconds: 25 + 25 + 30 + 25 + 40 + 35**.

### 1. 0:00–0:25 — source to reviewed rule

Open **View evidence** on **Move the $450 installment**. Point to the exact fictional quote, source version, and separate evidence, human-review and third-party approval fields. Say: “A source can suggest an option; it cannot silently authorize one.”

### 2. 0:25–0:50 — nominal plan and trace

The nominal plan moves the same $450 installment from September 13 to September 26. Lowest balance becomes **$50** instead of **−$400**, while ending balance remains **$500**. Open **Why this plan?** and say: “Timing relief, not savings. Deterministic integer-cent code computes the ledger; the model never writes a balance.”

### 3. 0:50–1:20 — hold the plan fixed and break it

Under **Verify plan**, choose **Payday through Sep 28** and run **Verify fixed plan**. Show **Unsafe**, complete **8/8** coverage, the September 27 payday witness, first shortfall September 26, and **−$400**. The saved actions and dates did not change; this is bounded verification, not re-optimization.

### 4. 1:20–1:45 — diagnose the cash hole

Press **Explain cash gap**. Show **$400 · Proven fixed-schedule buffer** and the one-cent witness. Say: “This repairs this fixed schedule across these eight declared cases; it is a diagnostic, not funding.” Cash cannot repair missing evidence or denied approval.

### 5. 1:45–2:25 — honest resilient alternative

First press **Find verified alternative** on the original example. It returns **No solution in this declared domain**; do not shrink the bounds. Then press **Open resilient example**, choose **Payday Sep 3–5**, verify the saved schedule is Unsafe, and search again. The separate fictional example returns a fixed September 5 option with a $1 fee and an independent **SAFE 3/3** proof. Press **Adopt verified schedule** and show **$49** plus **Verified Safe**. Adoption saves a plan and proof together but executes nothing externally.

### 6. 2:25–3:00 — clause to cash consequence

Press **Open original example**, then **Compare option alone** for **Cancel phone service**. Follow Source → Reviewed rule → Dependency → Proposed effect → Cash consequence. The $60 service charge is removed, but the same existing $480 device debt moves from November 20 to September 1 exactly once. The read-only result is **−$820 minimum / $80 ending**, versus the saved **$50 / $500**. Close with: “ClauseGraph makes the hidden consequence auditable before anyone acts.”

## Judge answers

- **Is SAFE a proof?** Within the displayed finite bounds and horizon, with complete coverage, yes. It says nothing outside them. A cutoff without a witness is Unknown.
- **Did a model compute the money?** No. Provider extraction can create typed candidates, but deterministic code with integer cents computes every cash result.
- **Is $400 funding?** No. `is_funding` is false. No money was obtained and no obligation changed.
- **Did you turn the failed original into a success?** No. The original exhausts its permitted domain with no solution. The successful schedule is a separately labelled three-document synthetic example.
- **Is the $60 cancellation a saving?** Not by itself. The same $480 device obligation accelerates, making the shown cash path worse.
- **Was this spoken in three minutes?** Not yet unless a human entry exists in `rehearsal-log.md`. Automated functional timing is not narration timing.

## Do not say

- “Guaranteed” without naming the bounds and horizon.
- “Optimal resilient plan”; the search returns the first independently verified feasible schedule.
- “Savings” for a deferral or acceleration.
- “Live provider result” during the synthetic fallback.
- “Presentation-ready” until a human spoken run is recorded.
